# Intelligence Flashcards

## Card 1

**Front:** What is the fundamental definition of intelligence in the context of intelligent systems?

**Back:** The ability to acquire and apply knowledge to learn from experience, solve problems, and interact with the environment.

---

## Card 2

**Front:** According to the source, what are the two core components used to decompose an intelligent agent?

**Back:** Knowledge representation and reasoning.

---

## Card 3

**Front:** Term: Knowledge representation

**Back:** Definition: The language AI systems use to structure, encode, and communicate information about a domain.

---

## Card 4

**Front:** Term: Reasoning

**Back:** Definition: The cognitive process of analyzing information, applying rules, and drawing conclusions based on evidence or premises.

---

## Card 5

**Front:** What is the trade-off typically encountered when choosing a knowledge representation?

**Back:** The trade-off between how expressive the representation is and how efficiently it can be processed.

---

## Card 6

**Front:** According to Geoff Hulten, what characterizes an 'intelligent system'?

**Back:** A system that connects users to AI/ML to achieve objectives and evolves/improves by watching user interactions.

---

## Card 7

**Front:** What are the two main categories of intelligent systems based on their role relative to the user?

**Back:** Intelligent autonomous systems and intelligent advisor systems (IASs).

---

## Card 8

**Front:** How does an intelligent autonomous system differ from an intelligent advisor system regarding decision-making?

**Back:** An autonomous system performs tasks and makes decisions independently, while an advisor system provides recommendations for the user.

---

## Card 9

**Front:** Which category of intelligent system is characterized by decision support, context awareness, and user interaction?

**Back:** Intelligent advisor systems (IASs).

---

## Card 10

**Front:** What is the primary objective of an intelligent advisor system (IAS)?

**Back:** To support and enhance a user's decision-making capabilities rather than replacing their expertise.

---

## Card 11

**Front:** List three features of intelligent autonomous systems.

**Back:** Full automation, real-time decision-making, and adaptability to changing environments.

---

## Card 12

**Front:** In an intelligent system, what is the purpose of the 'feedback loop'?

**Back:** To fine-tune knowledge representations using data observations or human feedback (such as RLHF).

---

## Card 13

**Front:** What does RLHF stand for in the context of aligning intelligent agents with user preferences?

**Back:** Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback.

---

## Card 14

**Front:** The characteristic of 'Intelligent experience' requires the system to achieve outcomes while minimizing _____ when mistakes occur.

**Back:** costs

---

## Card 15

**Front:** Which design characteristic involves managing multiple algorithms and tools where the output of one becomes the input for another?

**Back:** Orchestration

---

## Card 16

**Front:** Term: Knowledge acquisition

**Back:** Definition: The process of collecting information from data sources, the environment, or experts and converting it into structured representations.

---

## Card 17

**Front:** How is knowledge representation typically structured in Knowledge Graphs (KGs)?

**Back:** Explicitly, using nodes for entities and edges for relationships.

---

## Card 18

**Front:** How is knowledge representation typically structured in Large Language Models (LLMs)?

**Back:** Implicitly, as statistical patterns encoded in dense, high-dimensional vector spaces.

---

## Card 19

**Front:** What is a major advantage of explicit knowledge representation in KGs?

**Back:** It is directly interpretable by both humans and machines.

---

## Card 20

**Front:** Why is knowledge in LLMs considered 'opaque' compared to KGs?

**Back:** It is stored as billions of numerical parameters, making it difficult for humans to inspect or modify.

---

## Card 21

**Front:** What is required to update knowledge in an LLM, making it more complex than updating a KG?

**Back:** Retraining or fine-tuning the model.

---

## Card 22

**Front:** Which reasoning type begins with a general statement or hypothesis to reach a specific, logical conclusion?

**Back:** Deductive reasoning

---

## Card 23

**Front:** Which reasoning type makes broad generalizations based on specific observations or patterns in data?

**Back:** Inductive reasoning

---

## Card 24

**Front:** What is a major limitation of purely deductive reasoning engines in real-world applications?

**Back:** They require a highly complete and accurate knowledge base, which is rarely available.

---

## Card 25

**Front:** How does inductive reasoning (powered by ML) address the limitations of deductive reasoning?

**Back:** It enables generalization from incomplete data and handles inference under uncertainty.

---

## Card 26

**Front:** In the 'Farmer and Sheep' example, why did Claude.ai fail to solve the simpler version correctly?

**Back:** It relied on probabilistic matching of the prompt to a more complex well-known version from its training data.

---

## Card 27

**Front:** What role do LLMs play when integrated with KG-based reasoning engines in a hybrid system?

**Back:** They act as a reasoning layer that interprets ambiguous inputs and provides contextually relevant suggestions.

---

## Card 28

**Front:** What does a 'bottom-up' approach to KG construction focus on initially?

**Back:** Importing and consolidating all available data sources before considering functional tasks.

---

## Card 29

**Front:** Why do bottom-up approaches to KG adoption often fail?

**Back:** Because of excessive data source complexity and the lack of alignment with specific business objectives.

---

## Card 30

**Front:** What drives the 'purpose-driven' approach to KG construction?

**Back:** Business objectives and specific functional tasks required by the end user.

---

## Card 31

**Front:** Which methodology is revisited in the text to guide the development of intelligent systems based on KGs?

**Back:** CRISP-DM (Cross-Industry Standard Process for Data Mining).

---

## Card 32

**Front:** In the CRISP-DM cycle for KGs, what drives the 'Data understanding' phase?

**Back:** The business understanding and defined goals.

---

## Card 33

**Front:** Term: Schemaless

**Back:** Definition: A database or data structure flexibility that allows storing items with few or no formatting constraints.

---

## Card 34

**Front:** Concept: Predictive model

**Back:** Definition: A formula for estimating an unknown target value based on the results of a learning process on training data.

---

## Card 35

**Front:** How do LLMs assist in the 'Data preparation' phase of a hybrid IAS?

**Back:** By extracting relevant entities and relationships from unstructured data sources.

---

## Card 36

**Front:** What is the result of 'Deductive' reasoning if the premises are true?

**Back:** The conclusion is logically and necessarily true.

---

## Card 37

**Front:** Why is 'Inductive' reasoning often referred to as 'inductive inference'?

**Back:** Because it involves the ability to label unseen examples by generalizing from seen examples.

---

## Card 38

**Front:** What determines the requirements for defining the content and structure of a KG in a purpose-driven approach?

**Back:** The specific portion of data needed to reach the business goals.

---

## Card 39

**Front:** In the architecture of an intelligent system, what component converts data into a format suitable for the reasoning engine?

**Back:** Knowledge acquisition

---

## Card 40

**Front:** What outcome does the reasoning engine produce in an intelligent advisor system (IAS)?

**Back:** Insights, suggestions, and recommendations.

---

## Card 41

**Front:** Which type of reasoning is better suited for precise, rule-based tasks?

**Back:** Knowledge Graph (KG) based reasoning.

---

## Card 42

**Front:** Which type of reasoning is better for tasks involving pattern recognition and handling ambiguity?

**Back:** Large Language Model (LLM) based reasoning.

---

## Card 43

**Front:** The definition of an intelligent system by Geoff Hulten emphasizes the pivotal role of the _____.

**Back:** user

---

## Card 44

**Front:** In a medical IAS, what kind of data might be included in the knowledge base besides patient records?

**Back:** Research literature, medical best practices, and genomic datasets.

---

## Card 45

**Front:** What is the primary function of the 'Inference engine' in an inductive reasoner?

**Back:** To abstract and generalize from the knowledge base to provide suggestions under uncertainty.

---

## Card 46

**Front:** True or False: LLMs inherently possess common-sense reasoning capabilities comparable to humans.

**Back:** False.

---

## Card 47

**Front:** How do hybrid systems use KGs to improve LLM performance?

**Back:** KGs provide up-to-date, structured data to mitigate hallucinations and ensure factual precision.

---

## Card 48

**Front:** What is the 'Orchestration' phase responsible for in the lifecycle of an intelligent system?

**Back:** Controlling risk, maintaining quality, and managing how the system acquires knowledge from sources.

---

## Card 49

**Front:** In a deductive reasoner, how many directions does communication typically flow between the reasoner and the knowledge base?

**Back:** One direction (reasoner reads from the knowledge base but rarely stores results back in it).

---

## Card 50

**Front:** What is the starting point for an inductive reasoning process?

**Back:** Specific observations or data samples of reality.

---

## Card 51

**Front:** In a hybrid approach, what provides the 'contextualized information' for specific answers?

**Back:** The combination of global generic knowledge (LLMs) and structured domain knowledge (KGs).

---

## Card 52

**Front:** What is the 'Black Box' analogy as applied to intelligent systems?

**Back:** From the outside, an intelligent system accomplishes complex tasks, but its internal functions require decomposition to understand.

---

## Card 53

**Front:** Why is the schemaless nature of graph databases beneficial for iterative development?

**Back:** It allows for extensions with new node and relationship types without compromising existing functionality.

---

## Card 54

**Front:** What is the core argument for using KGs as fundamental data structures in AI?

**Back:** They allow for efficient representation, insight derivation, and the groundwork for precise reasoning.

---

## Card 55

**Front:** Which component of an intelligent system is responsible for 'sensing' the world through data ingestion?

**Back:** Knowledge acquisition

---

## Card 56

**Front:** In Hulten's definition, how does an intelligent system evolve?

**Back:** By watching how users interact with the system.

---

## Card 57

**Front:** How does a 'Full automation' system differ from an IAS in its 'Act' phase?

**Back:** Full automation acts without human input; IAS suggests actions, but the user makes the final decision.

---

## Card 58

**Front:** What role does 'Domain expert' play in the knowledge acquisition of an LLM?

**Back:** Selecting data sources, evaluating results, and providing custom fine-tuning.

---

## Card 59

**Front:** What is the main effort in the knowledge acquisition process for LLMs?

**Back:** Selecting and cleaning vast amounts of unstructured data sources.

---

## Card 60

**Front:** True or False: Reasoning engines in an intelligent system can operate in sequence or in parallel.

**Back:** True.

---

## Card 61

**Front:** In the medical diagnosis scenario, what does a 'Deductive reasoner' use to infer optimal actions?

**Back:** Probabilistic relationships, costs, and patient preferences encoded in the knowledge base.

---

## Card 62

**Front:** What shift in reasoning does the 'hybrid paradigm' embrace?

**Back:** Reasoning is no longer just formal inference but includes probabilistic, contextual, and pattern-based computations.

---

## Card 63

**Front:** What is the 'Target' in a predictive model?

**Back:** The unknown value of interest that the model is designed to estimate.

---

## Card 64

**Front:** Why is domain-specific understanding preferred over generic knowledge in professional IASs?

**Back:** To provide accurate, expert-refined support rather than generic answers.

---

## Card 65

**Front:** In a KG approach, what defines the 'semantics' of the data?

**Back:** Ontologies or schemas.

---

## Card 66

**Front:** What happens in the 'Modeling' phase of the revisited CRISP-DM for KGs?

**Back:** Defining algorithms for machine learning tasks and testing predictive models.

---

## Card 67

**Front:** How does an IAS handle user preferences or specific scenarios?

**Back:** Through context awareness, which tailors advice to the current situation.

---

## Card 68

**Front:** Name one field where IASs are commonly used for identifying patterns or fraud.

**Back:** Financial services.

---

## Card 69

**Front:** The process of transforming raw data into a structured format for a KG often involves a(n) _____ to ensure accuracy.

## Card 71

**Front:** The ability to reason with 'ambiguous' knowledge is a strength of which component?

**Back:** Large Language Models (LLMs).

---

## Card 78

**Back:** A structured storage of converted knowledge suitable for the system's reasoning engines.

---

**Front:** Why is the 'meaningful objective' considered the driver of the entire development process?

**Back:** It ensures the system exists for a specific, achievable purpose that matters to the end users.
